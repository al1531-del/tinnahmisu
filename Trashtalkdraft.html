No More Sourpatches

This is a box of Sour Patch Kids Christmas editions. It only came in red and green flavors because it’s the Christmas edition. I got this as a gift because it was Christmas. This was from my friend for Secret Santa last year. My wonderful and generous friend got me a lot of candy, and I love them all.

I am throwing this away because, unfortunately, my friends and I have finished the box of Sour Patch Kids. There were originally some Sour Patch Kids remaining, and that is because I prefer the red ones, but everybody else prefers the green ones.

One instance where we ate a lot of Sour Patch Kids was when we were watching a sad, depressing Netflix show called “Adolescence” at 1 o’clock AM, and we downed half the box because we got sad and depressed. Then, coincidentally, there were also red and green Jell-O shots, so we drank those while eating the Sour Patches.

I really like Sour Patches because I prefer sour candies over the super sweet ones or the ones that taste like nothing (ahem Twizzlers and candy corns). I like how it balances the sourness and a little bit of sweetness.

Wired Hairtie Gone Awry

This is a hairtie I had as a child (like since I was around seven years old). I bought this in a fashion store in China, where I bought so many hair accessories, such as hair ties, headbands, hair clips, combs, etc. It was really a dream come true for kid me, and I loved looking around the stores and the cute decorations.

I liked to use this for when I braided my hair because the hair band is smaller than the usual hairtie. I still used it for ponytails and buns, but it’s a little tight. I probably used this more for ponytails when I was younger, when my head was smaller and probably had less hair too. I liked this because it had a cute plaid bow as decoration. I would rotate it so the bow would be in the front after I did my hair.

I unfortunately have to throw this away because the wire inside the plaid bow is poking out of the bow, and it pokes you when you try to use it, and it’s annoying. Sometimes my hair gets stuck in it, too. Despite this, I still used it in the recent past because I lose track of my hairties all the time and grab the nearest one available.

No Longer Juicy

This is an empty plastic travel-sized bottle labeled “Juicy” from the fashion brand “Juicy Couture.” This came as part of the travel-sized makeup/vanity bag. I needed a bigger one than the one I had to fit all my makeup and self-care stuff in, so I bought one that came at a good price and quality and size.

To my surprise, it came with this bottle as well. I don’t usually travel, and when I do, I just use my travel-size shampoo or conditioner. I do not feel the need to pour it into another bottle, but I understand how that might be more sustainable. Overall, I just do not have any particular need for this item. If anything, I just bring the bottles I’m already using and call it a day.


I am throwing this away because I have no need for this and it is taking up space. There’s a special kind of inconvenience when you have empty bottles– especially plastic ones– just lying around because the instant you lay the lightest touch on it, it goes flying off the table and knocks other things down all the way. In the name of the organization, this plastic Juicy Couture bottle has to go.

Coda

Thanks for coming by. I hope you found each anecdote interesting. Taking a step back and looking at the things I’ve deemed worth throwing out gave me a clear picture of who I am as a person, based on what I choose to consume and discard. If Knock-off Fluttershy didn’t reek of plastic, would I have thrown her away simply because she was a bootleg version of the original? Couldn’t I have just fixed the wired hair tie?
I hope this also raises the question of whether we are the person we are today because of the things we consume, aka the things we buy. To what extent does that influence our perception of ourselves? Hopefully, this inspires anyone who’s stuck around to do the same.
